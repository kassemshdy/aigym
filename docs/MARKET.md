# Market research

Researched September 2026 across the three categories this product straddles.

## Gym and studio management

Mindbody, Glofox, PushPress, Zen Planner, Gymdesk. Strong on billing, scheduling, and
check-in kiosks — Mindbody links recurring billing to check-ins, Zen Planner ships a
dedicated kiosk mode, PushPress triggers invoices from membership status. Weak on what
happens *during* the session.

## Coaching platforms

Everfit, Trainerize, TrueCoach. Everfit leads on UI, automation, and multi-language, with
the most complete single-app client experience. Trainerize leads on integrations and
studio-scale features, but its 2026 check-in forms are widely reported as fragmented —
coaches still export to Typeform or JotForm. TrueCoach is simplest for 1:1 up to ~50
clients. List pricing runs ~$105/mo (Everfit) to ~$248/mo (Trainerize), with real bills
40–60% higher once nutrition, automation, payments, and branding are added.

## AI fitness

Fitbod generates adaptive workouts from a 1,000+ exercise library, no nutrition or
coaching layer. Freeletics adapts on post-session difficulty ratings. Trainera bundles AI
plans, macro tracking, food-photo logging, and an optional human coach. The consistent 2026
finding: **adaptive quality beats library size** — plans should adjust at the day level
from goals, load history, schedule friction, and recovery signals, not emit a static week.

## Where AIGym fits

No competitor joins all four of: the front desk (dues, check-in), the floor (coach-held set
logging and a spoken calorie check), the content (the gym's *own* coach's videos), and
adaptive AI.

More to the point, every platform above is priced and built for a US gym on fibre:
cloud-only apps that white-screen during a power cut, no Arabic, and monthly pricing that
is a large share of a small Beirut gym's revenue. The competition here is a WhatsApp group
and a notebook. That is the wedge: **works offline, in Arabic, on the phone you already
own, at a price a Lebanese gym can pay.**
