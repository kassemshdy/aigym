# Go to market

Written for Triple A Gym, Aaramoun. The frames below are Alex Hormozi's — the value
equation, the conditional guarantee, the one-constraint idea — applied to this business.
They come from his books and talks; none of it is advice he has given about this company.

## The constraint right now is proof, not money and not product

There is a working product and zero paying gyms. That ordering matters: a deck built on a
prototype is an idea deck, and idea decks in a small market raise badly. The same four
weeks spent getting real usage turn the identical deck into a traction deck.

The unfair advantage is that **we own the customer.** Triple A is a live lab — first
customer, test bed and case study at once. Most founders in this category have to guess what
a gym floor needs.

## The offer

Hormozi's value equation is *(dream outcome × perceived likelihood) ÷ (time delay × effort)*.

| Lever | The move |
|---|---|
| Dream outcome | "Collect every dollar you are owed, and stop losing members quietly." Not "gym software." |
| Perceived likelihood | Triple A's own before/after numbers. **This is the missing piece, and it is the whole game.** |
| Time delay | Live in 48 hours. We import their members from the notebook ourselves. |
| Effort & sacrifice | Staff tap; they never type. The product already works this way. |

**The guarantee:** *"If it does not recover more in missed dues than we charge you in the
first 90 days, you do not pay."*

Conditional, not blanket — the kind that actually converts. It is affordable because the
lapsed-member list and dues tracking drive that number directly, and it forces us to build
toward the outcome the gym owner actually cares about.

## Pricing

Price against what a notebook loses them, not against Trainerize's feature list. A gym that
recovers a handful of memberships it would otherwise have lost has paid for the year.
Anchoring on features invites a comparison we do not need to win.

## The next 90 days

**Days 1–30 — prove it at home.** Run Triple A entirely on the app: front desk, plus Karim
and Abed on the floor. Record three numbers before and after:

1. Dues collected within 7 days of the due date
2. Members who went 14+ days without visiting
3. Time to register a new member

**Days 31–60 — three to five paying pilot gyms.** Warm outreach and in-person visits. Charge
a real if small amount: a free pilot teaches nothing about willingness to pay, which is the
only thing still unknown.

**Days 61–90 — package the proof.** Case study from our own gym plus pilot numbers. That is
the traction slide, and the point at which raising makes sense.

## Where Hormozi's playbook does not transfer

His lead-generation advice assumes a large, ad-addressable market — the "rule of 100" is
built for that. Lebanon has a few hundred serious gyms. The volume game does not apply;
walking into gyms does. Treat the offer and guarantee frames as the transferable part, and
the channel advice as something to adapt rather than copy.
